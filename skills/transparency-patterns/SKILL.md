# Transparency Patterns

## Use this when

People need enough visibility to make a good decision with an AI system, rather than being asked to trust a result they cannot inspect or challenge.

## Show what matters

Make the following visible when they affect the person’s decision:

- the information the system used, inferred or could not access;
- what it has done and what it will do next;
- confidence, uncertainty and important limitations;
- the source of a material claim or recommendation;
- the route to inspect more detail, correct the record or ask for review.

Match the explanation to the stakes and the person’s expertise. A quick, low-stakes task may need a short signal. A consequential decision needs clear sources, limits and a route to challenge the result.

## Avoid false clarity

Do not present an uncertain inference as a fact. Do not hide limitations in fine print. Do not expose a long internal trace when a clear explanation of the source, uncertainty and next action would be more useful.

## Check before you continue

Confirm that a person can tell what the system knows, what it does not know, what it has done, and what they can do next.

## Workshop use

Use this with the operating agreement and workflow map when completing “What the person needs to know” and “How a person can intervene.” It also strengthens the research-synthesis starting challenge where claims need traceable evidence.
