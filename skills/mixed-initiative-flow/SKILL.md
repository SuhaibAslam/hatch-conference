# Mixed-Initiative Flow

## Purpose

Make agency explicit in the working specification and workflow. A person and an AI system can both contribute to the work; the design needs to show who leads, when control shifts and how the person can intervene.

## Map the work

For each meaningful workflow step, identify:

- who leads: person, AI system or shared;
- the action being proposed, prepared or performed;
- the information and permissions the system relies on;
- what the person needs to see;
- the handoff trigger that changes who leads;
- the route to pause, edit, redirect, approve or recover.

Treat high stakes, subjective judgment, consent, rights and material cost as reasons to make human authority visible and easy to exercise.

## Design the handoff

Use clear handoffs. The system can propose options, wait for direction, ask for approval or return work when it lacks the authority or evidence to continue. Record why a consequential action changed state so a person can understand and challenge it later.

Avoid rapid, unclear transfers of control. A person should be able to tell whose turn it is, what the system has done and what remains possible.

Use this skill with Team Canvas 2 and Team Canvas 3. It supports the choices that stay with people, what people need to see, how they can question or stop the work, where authority changes, and how work resumes after a problem.
