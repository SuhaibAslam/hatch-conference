# Decision Evidence Brief

1. State the decision the team needs to make.
2. Separate observed evidence from interpretation and missing evidence.
3. Identify the issue with the most meaningful consequence.
4. Recommend one next action and explain why it is proportionate to the evidence.
5. End with the human decision that still needs to be made.

Do not turn an untested explanation into a fact. Preserve meaningful disagreement or uncertainty.

Output A

Onboarding flow recommendation

The permission explanation appears to be the main problem in the onboarding flow. Users may find the copy confusing or too long.

  • Shorten the permission copy and make the benefit clearer.
  • Add a tooltip or example so users understand why access is needed.
  • Run another usability test after updating the screen.

This should reduce confusion and improve completion.

Output B

Decision brief

Decision: Choose what to test next before changing the permission step.

Observed evidence: 4 of 6 participants missed the permission explanation. Two later completed the flow after retrying.

Interpretation: The permission step is the clearest current friction. The notes do not show whether wording, sequence or another factor caused it.

Recommended next action: Test 2 small variants, one with shorter copy and one with the explanation moved earlier. Keep the rest of the flow stable and compare comprehension.

Human decision: Decide which explanation the product can accurately promise before either variant is produced.
